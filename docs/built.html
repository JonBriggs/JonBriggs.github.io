Built

Software made for the real work of schools. It's where the thinking gets tested.

I've written most of this software myself, with real help on design and on the migrations from one version to the next. Nearly every piece started the same way: a colleague had a problem that no product quite solved, not because the products were bad, but because a school is more specific than the average one any vendor builds for. Most of it is still running at Eastside Prep, which is the only review that counts.

Four11

The information system behind EPS — courses, sections, enrollments, teaching loads, scheduling, advising, and grades. It began, at about 80 students, as a way to stop printing end-of-term comments and hand-sorting them into envelopes; some of that first code still runs.

An LTI experiment in classroom gamification: students earned points, opened chests, and collected items to dress up an avatar — the whole wardrobe rendered as layered SVGs. Playful, and genuinely fun to use.

retired

Aggregated each student's assignments across all their classes and graphed them, reading special Canvas comment codes — M: for missing, INC: for incomplete. Retired when it wasn't used enough to justify the upkeep; a candidate to revive.

Programs & infrastructure

Not software, but the institutional work underneath it — programs and systems I've led or helped build at Eastside Prep over two decades. The fuller story, era by era, is in History of Tech @ Eastside Prep ↗.

since 2005
Upper School 1:1 Laptop Program

My first project as Director of Technology was choosing tablet PCs for the first Upper School students; twenty donated machines started a program that has put a laptop in every Upper School student's hands since.

2012
Middle School 1:1 Laptop Program

Extending the one-to-one model into the Middle School, age-appropriately, in the same fall as Canvas — one of the transitions that shaped how EPS teaches.

2012
Canvas Rollout

Led the adoption of Canvas as the school's learning management system, replacing EPSnet, our homegrown SharePoint system from 2005 — selection, migration, and getting the faculty genuinely using it. EPS was among the first dozen K–12 schools anywhere on Canvas.

since 2012
Makerspace

It started with a laser cutter in the fabrication studio, before “makerspace” was much of a word, and grew into a purpose-built space in the TMAC building and then one sized for the Middle School. I designed the tools, the layout, and the program that grew up around them.

2011–2017
TEDxEastsidePrep

I started and ran the school's TEDx event for seven years, with Jennifer Cross on the team.

since 2008
Campus Technology Infrastructure

Until 2008 it was equipment bought on eBay and cobbled together. The Head asked for a three-year plan; the money came faster, so the whole plan went in over one summer: real Wi-Fi, projectors in every classroom, new switches and servers. Ten-gigabit links between every building followed with TALI in 2018, as the school grew past 500 students.
