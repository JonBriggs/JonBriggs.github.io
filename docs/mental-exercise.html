Mental Exercise

Inspire · Fall 2024

An essay on the felt experience of hard thinking — and why, as our tools make it easier to avoid, schools should name that experience rather than smooth it away.

It takes the ninth-grade wind sprint as its figure for intellectual effort: the coach can watch you skate, but only the runner knows whether they reached ninety-five percent or a true hundred. Thinking is the same. The discomfort of cognitive dissonance, the frustration that precedes insight, the particular absorption of flow — these are the signs that real work is happening, and they are largely invisible from the outside.

The argument is that teachers should talk about that inner experience directly with students, because the tools now make it trivial to produce the outward product without ever doing the inward work. Naming the discomfort is how you teach someone to seek it out instead of routing around it.
